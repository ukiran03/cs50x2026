sort1 uses: Bubble Sort

How do you know?: It exhibits O(N^2) time on random/reversed data, but drops
drastically to O(N) linear time on already sorted data due to early-exit
optimization.

sort2 uses: Merge Sort

How do you know?: It is the fastest overall and scales logarithmically/linearly
O(NlogN), Its performance remains highly consistent across random, reversed,
sorted data.

sort3 uses: Selection Sort

How do you know?: It scales quadratically O(N^2), but unlike Bubble sort, its
execution time remains identical regardless of whether the input is random,
reversed, or already sorted.
